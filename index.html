About Me

// about me video

This portfolio documents where I am in my career, what I've built, and how I think — put together as part of COOP 2100 at York University's Lassonde School of Engineering and kept as a living record going forward.

I'm Klodiana Kamberi (she/her), a third-year Computer Science student at York University's Lassonde School of Engineering. My program covers software engineering, systems, and applied development — and I've tried to push that into practice wherever I can, from university research labs to a real QA internship.

I gravitated toward mobile development because building an app is the most direct way to write code that interacts with an operating system in something people can actually hold and use. Kotlin made that click for me — and Kotlin Multiplatform means I'm not locked to one platform. A real device, with real gestures and real constraints, is a different problem than building for the web. That difference is what keeps me interested.

Mobile development with Kotlin Multiplatform and Jetpack Compose. Cross-platform experiences that feel native on every device.

// background

Previously a Research Assistant at York's Social Robotics Lab — built automation pipelines, webhook integrations, and a ROS2 motor control node.

// reflection

When I started this degree I had a general interest in computers but no real direction. Focusing on mobile felt like a narrowing at the time — it was actually the opposite. It forced me to understand how software interacts with hardware, how UI decisions affect performance, and how to think about users as people with real expectations. Working in QA sharpened that further. You can't test software well without understanding it deeply, and you can't understand it deeply without caring about how it behaves at the edges. That mindset now carries into everything I build.

The QA internship taught me things a classroom couldn't. I expected to learn about testing tools and bug tracking — I did — but what I didn't expect was how much the role would develop my ability to communicate across a team. Finding a bug is only half the work; explaining it clearly, reproducing it reliably, and working with developers to fix it without friction is the other half. Personal Development Goals

The clearest gap I identified in my self-assessment was around visibility and communication — I do the work, but I don't naturally surface it. So the most concrete thing I'm working on is a weekly Friday summary to my manager: what was tested, what broke, what's outstanding. It sounds simple, but writing it consistently forces me to synthesize my week rather than just move through it. Twenty-five of those by December 2026 is the target.

On the technical side, I want to go deeper into why bugs happen rather than just what happened. That means reading Kotlin source code to trace root causes, not just logging symptoms. I'm also working through a Spring Boot and KMP course — one module per week — connecting each piece to what I'm actually doing in the internship, not treating it as abstract study.

The goal I'm least comfortable with but most committed to: actively advocating for bug prioritization in sprint planning. I know what I find, I know why it matters, and I need to make that case out loud instead of assuming it's obvious. That starts in July 2026 and applies to every session from there.

— VALUES & PERSONALITY —

Values & Personality

I value work that is clear in its purpose and honest in its feedback. The QA role reinforced this: the whole job is to surface truth about a product, even when it's inconvenient. I value competence over credentials — the experiences that taught me the most were ones where I had to figure things out rather than follow a guide.

The person who shaped me most growing up was my mother. She handles difficult situations with composure, never needs to announce how capable she is, and just gets things done. Watching her taught me that reliability matters more than brilliance on its own — showing up and not falling apart when things get hard are the things that actually carry you through.

I am introverted — I don't naturally make my work visible unless there's a clear reason to. When I stay engaged and communicate regularly, things go well. When I go quiet, performance drops. Recognizing that pattern is part of why Goals 5 and 6 exist.
